Full deep read

Multiomics, functional experiments and insurance claims prioritize Alzheimer’s disease targets and drugs; clinical preventive effects remain unproven.

The authors

Yuan Hou, Yichen Li and Pengyue Zhang are joint first authors; Feixiong Cheng at Cleveland Clinic is the corresponding author. The collaboration combines genetic analysis, cellular and animal experiments, and claims-based pharmacoepidemiology. The article was published on 8 October 2026.

The problem

Which Alzheimer’s disease (AD) genetic signals can inform interventions? The study prioritizes genetically supported targets, experimentally investigates EPHX2, and tests associations between existing medications and incident AD among people with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). These branches address related but distinct questions.

The approach

The genetic screen starts with 1,229 druggable targets and instruments from three pQTL and nine eQTL datasets. Instrument selection requires FDR < 0.05, F > 10 and LD pruning. Variants associated with more than two proteins or transcripts are excluded; this filter alone cannot establish the absence of horizontal pleiotropy.

The alzMR score sums signed Z statistics across xQTL–AD GWAS combinations. Its sign suggests inhibition or activation as a therapeutic direction. Colocalization PP.H4 > 0.5 and fine-mapping PIP > 0.5 provide candidate support. The score magnitude is not a treatment-effect estimate.

The EPHX2 branch uses patient-derived neurons, cerebral organoids, variant editing and EC5026 treatment in 5xFAD mice. The claims branch includes 111,680 Optum patients with MCI and screens 210 drugs using propensity-score matching and Cox models. Four exposure definitions compare any versus no exposure, two definitions of higher versus lower exposure, and continuous medication possession ratio (MPR). A separate MarketScan analysis compares trazodone with escitalopram.

Key results

The authors prioritize 19 targets in European ancestry and seven suggestive targets in African ancestry. The latter require P < 0.05 in at least three xQTL–GWAS pairs; this is distinct from the European-ancestry FDR criterion.

The EPHX2 IVW estimate is β = 0.190 (95% CI 0.121–0.260). Experiments with p.Arg287Gln reduce pTau. EC5026 improves novel-object recognition (P = 0.014) and contextual fear memory (P = 0.033) in mice. These experiments do not test prevention in humans.

Twelve drugs meet FDR < 0.05 and HR < 1 in all four claims models. Higher versus lower trazodone exposure has HR = 0.86 (95% CI 0.80–0.91). In the independent database, comparison with escitalopram gives HR = 0.78 (P = 6.44 × 10⁻⁶; Figure 6d).

How solid is the evidence

EPHX2 has convergent genetic and experimental support. Overlapping GWAS participants, cells derived from one patient and small animal experiments limit independence and generalizability. The Cauchy combination test addresses correlated tests without adding independent cohorts.

The four claims models share participants and prescribing information. Indication, adherence and healthcare use can leave residual confounding. If MPR classification uses post-baseline information without aligning eligibility, exposure assignment and follow-up, time-related bias can arise. The supplementary methods must establish whether that condition applies; this review does not assert that the bias occurred. Claims diagnoses also lack routine biomarker confirmation.
