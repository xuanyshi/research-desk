GENESIS: Towards Explainable Causal Discovery

Abhinav Thorat, Ravi Kumar Kolla, Vishak K Bhat, Harsh Vardhan Singh Chauhan et al.

30-second read

GENESIS uses motifs, statistical checks, Markov blankets, and LLM adjudication to reach 100% decision-traceability coverage. That metric is guaranteed by the workflow, while appendix tables still contain fully justified incorrect edges.

Question
When LLMs assist causal discovery, can every edge decision remain inspectable without sacrificing structural accuracy?
Design
An arXiv version 1 preprint comparing statistical and LLM-assisted discovery on five benchmark Bayesian networks, three sample sizes, and six random seeds.
Evidence strength Limited
What was assessed
SHD, edge-level resolution sources, and correctness on five known graphs, at sample sizes 1,000, 2,500, and 5,000.
Downgrade triggers
If rationale coverage implied correctness, source-resolved edges should be almost uniformly correct; only 6 of 11 statistically resolved Child edges are correct, and every experiment uses a small synthetic benchmark.
Impact on interpretation
The study supports an edge-level audit-trail prototype but does not establish correct causal discovery in real biomedical data.

GENESIS edge-decision chain

Full deep read

GENESIS splits causal discovery into three-node motif generation, statistical checking, and edge resolution, while retaining a reason for every included edge. It reaches 100% "decision traceability" on five benchmark Bayesian networks, but that percentage is guaranteed by the workflow and does not mean that every edge is correct.

The authors

Abhinav Thorat, Ravi Kumar Kolla, Vishak K Bhat, Harsh Vardhan Singh Chauhan, and Niranjan Pedanekar are with the User Engagement Research team at Sony Research India. The paper was submitted to arXiv on 4 August 2026 and is currently a version 1 preprint.

The problem

Purely statistical causal discovery relies on conditional independence, score functions, or functional-form assumptions and often cannot orient edges under small samples or Markov equivalence. LLMs can add semantic priors from variable names and descriptions, but existing hybrids commonly return a final graph without showing why each edge was retained or removed.

The authors define decision traceability: every edge in the final DAG must retain at least one explicit justification from statistical evidence, Markov-blanket consistency, or LLM domain reasoning. This metric asks whether a reason can be inspected. It does not directly establish that the reason is sound or that the edge is true.

The approach

Stage 1, LLM Explorer, operates on variable metadata and three-node combinations. GPT-5 proposes chains, forks, and colliders with semantic confidence scores. Triggering a candidate motif turns isolated edges into a local structural hypothesis for later data checking.

Stage 2, Data Explorer, operates on candidate edges and motifs. Conditional-independence tests, BIC, additive-noise models, and LiNGAM are combined into edge confidence, while motif-implied dependence patterns are checked separately. Scores above a lower threshold enter the initial graph; intermediate scores or disagreement between the LLM and data trigger edge resolution.

Stage 3, Edge Resolution, intersects IAMB Markov blankets estimated from the data with blankets proposed by Qwen3.5-27B. Mutual membership retains an edge. Otherwise, GPT-5 enumerates five competing explanations - direct, two mediated alternatives, a longer chain, and a common cause - and Gemini 3 Pro returns KEEP or DISCARD. The final edge records its resolution source as an audit trail.

Key results

The experiments cover five Bayesian networks with known graphs: Asia, Cancer, Child, Earthquake, and Survey. The authors generate 1,000, 2,500, and 5,000 observations per network and repeat each setting across six random seeds. Structural Hamming Distance (SHD) is the main accuracy measure.

At 1,000 observations, GENESIS reports SHD values of 0.00, 0.17, and 0.33 on Earthquake, Cancer, and Asia, with 6.83 on Child and 4.17 on Survey. At 5,000 observations, the corresponding values are 0.00, 0.00, 1.00, 6.67, and 1.83. It beats PC, SCORE, NOTEARS, and DirectLiNGAM on most networks, but does not consistently beat LLM-CD, CausalOrder, or CaMML. On Child, LLM-CD or CaMML has lower SHD at every sample size.

Decision traceability is 100% in every setting because an edge cannot enter the final graph without at least one listed reason. Appendix edge tables also show source accuracy. On Child at n = 1,000, only 6 of 11 statistically resolved edges are correct and 11 of 12 Markov-blanket-resolved edges are correct. On Asia, 5 of 7 blanket-resolved edges are correct. Complete reason coverage can therefore coexist with incorrect edges.

How solid is the evidence

Evidence level: limited. Known graphs, three sample sizes, six repeats, and edge-level correctness tables support evaluation on small benchmarks. All observations come from five textbook networks, while node names and metadata expose semantic content. Medical labels in Asia and Cancer do not constitute real biomedical validation. There is no external real-world dataset, blinded metadata, distribution shift, latent-confounding test, or measurement-error test.

The 100% result is a process-coverage property created by the inclusion rule. It does not validate factual truth, independence of evidence sources, confidence calibration, or expert reproducibility. Using GPT-5, Qwen3.5-27B, and Gemini 3 Pro also makes performance depend on model versions, prompts, and service state.

Two internal inconsistencies affect reproduction. The Cancer example uses an uncertainty interval of 0.40 to 0.70, whereas the appendix hyperparameter table gives a lower threshold of 0.25. A methods footnote caps motifs at 5n, while the appendix says the maximum is fixed at 40 for all experiments. The algorithm declares a DAG output but does not explicitly show global cycle detection or conflicting-orientation resolution. Thresholds, candidate limits, and acyclicity handling need clarification.
